A quiz is written for a student, from the study material they are reading: a section, a whole material, or a whole
topic. It is a good quiz if:

- Its questions test whether the student understood the material: why something holds, how ideas relate, what
  follows from them, how they apply to a case. Not whether they remember its wording.
- Each question is answered by the section it cites, and only by what that section says: a student who understood
  the section can answer it without anything else.
- Exactly one choice is correct. The wrong choices are plausible to a student who misunderstood, and are wrong for a
  reason the section makes clear; none is a trick, a joke, or wrong only by a detail of wording.
- The questions cover the material evenly, its sections and its main ideas, rather than one part of it many times.
- Each question stands on its own, is clear, and gives nothing away to the other questions.
- It uses only the material it is given: nothing the material doesn't say.

The app reads the quiz as it is: each question, its choices, the index of the correct one, and the reference of the
section it cites (e.g. S3). A quiz is no use to the app unless every question has exactly the number of choices asked
for and cites a section by its reference.
